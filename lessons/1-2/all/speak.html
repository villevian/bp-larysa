Situation 1

1Client meeting. You are an event manager at BCN Smart Events. The client (your teacher) has a two-day conference in Barcelona, 14–15 May, and needs an evening event. Get the information, compare the venues, recommend one and agree on the plan.

🅰 You — event manager
  • Ask: number of guests, budget, special food, online guests.
  • Recommend one venue and explain why (cheaper than, the best option for …).
  • You have a 10% discount at the winery — try to sell it.
  • Finish with a clear next step.

Situation 2

2Negotiate with the caterer. The boat restaurant’s caterer sent a quote: €95 per person for 140 guests (€13,300). Your client’s budget is €8,000. Call the caterer (your teacher) and get a better price.

🅰 You
  • Explain the budget and the number of guests.
  • Ask what is in the price: drinks? service? the jazz band?
  • Suggest ways to make it cheaper: a buffet, fewer courses, a shorter drinks package, a smaller band.
  • Don’t accept the first offer. Your aim: €60 per person or less.
💬 Our budget is … · What’s included in the price? · That’s too expensive for us. · How about a buffet? · Can you do it for …? · If we …, can you …? · OK, it’s a deal.

Situation 3

3Brief your assistant. You have a 10-minute call with your new assistant (your teacher). The event is in three weeks. Explain what needs to be done, who does what and the deadlines.

🅰 You
  • Tasks: book the bus to the venue · send the guest list to the caterer · check the special menus (10 vegan, 4 gluten-free) · test the streaming for 25 online guests · print name badges.
  • Give each task a deadline.
  • Check that your assistant understands — ask him/her to repeat the plan.
💬 First, could you …? · The deadline is … · Please send … to … · Let me check: … · If you have a problem, call me. · No, not Thursday — Friday.

📝 Result: what did you agree?

Situation 4

4A problem with the venue. Two weeks before the event the client now has 170 guests, not 140. The venue takes 120–140. Call the venue manager (your teacher) and find a solution.

🅰 You
  • Explain the problem clearly.
  • Ask for options and compare them.
  • You can’t pay more than €1,000 extra.
  • Agree on a solution and on a date to confirm.
💬 We have a problem with … · Is it possible to …? · What are our options? · That’s more than our budget. · What’s the best you can do? · Let’s agree on …

📝 Result: what did you agree?

Situation 5

5After the event: the client complains. The day after the event the client (your teacher) calls you. She is not happy. Listen, apologise, explain and offer a solution — keep the client for next year.

🅰 You
  • Find out exactly what went wrong.
  • Apologise and explain (only real reasons, no excuses).
  • Offer something: a discount, a free extra next time, a refund for one part.
  • Agree on the next step and ask about next year.
💬 I’m very sorry about … · Can you tell me exactly what happened? · The reason was … · What we can do is … · We’d like to offer … · Can we work together next year?

📝 Result: what did you agree?

Situation 6

6Your own business. Now you are the client. Your company wants to organise an event for its clients (for example a seminar on new tax rules, an end-of-year evening or a team offsite). Brief an event agency (your teacher) with real information about your business.

🅰 You
  • Explain: who you are, what the event is for and who comes.
  • Give the facts: date, number of guests, place (in person / online / hybrid), budget.
  • Say what is most important for you and what you don’t want.
  • Ask the agency for two options and a price by a date.
💬 We are a … company. · The aim of the event is to … · We expect about … guests. · Our budget is about … · For us, the most important thing is … · Could you send us two options by …?

📝 Result: what did you agree?
